Search the recipe sites I trust

Five cooks’ sites and Kenji’s videos, in one search. Every result opens on the original site.

    Prototype with real sample data, pulled November 1, 2026: . The full index would hold about 4,500 to 5,500 recipes plus Kenji’s whole channel. No photos here: this page can’t load images from other sites, and whether to show them is still open.

    How it works

    No Google. A weekly job builds our own small index from sites that allow it, and search runs in your browser. It costs nothing to run.

    1. A list of sources, kept in the repo

      Five recipe sites, Kenji’s YouTube channel, and links for sites we don’t index.

    2. A polite weekly crawl

      Checks each site’s robots.txt, reads its sitemap, and fetches only recipes that are new or changed. One request at a time, a few seconds apart, with a clear name and contact page.

    3. Kenji’s videos through the YouTube API

      The official, free API lists every upload. Refreshed each week, well inside YouTube’s 30-day rule, and always labeled as YouTube.

    4. Ingredients matched with KitchenCatalog

      “Scallions”, “green onions” and “spring onion” all become green onion, so one search finds them all, and “Use what I have” can compare recipes to your pantry.

    5. A static index on each deploy

      The data becomes a search index your browser loads in small pieces. No server, no database.

    6. Search, then link out

      Results show only what you need to choose. The recipe itself is always on the original site.

    What we keep

    Kept

    • Title and link
    • Ingredient names
    • Total time, cuisine, course
    • Photo link, if we show photos

    Never kept

    • The method or steps
    • Headnotes and stories
    • Comments or ratings text
    • Anything from sites that say no
